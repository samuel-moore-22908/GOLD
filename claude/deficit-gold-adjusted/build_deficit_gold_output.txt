THE TRADE DEFICIT, REPORTED AND WITH THE GOLD TAKEN OUT
==============================================================================
   BOPGSTB, the FT-900 goods-and-services balance, 415 months,
   Jan 1992 to Jul 2026. Negative is a deficit.
   Gold covers 26 months, Oct 2023 to Nov 2025, all partners.

==============================================================================
STEP 1  The months everyone reacted to
==============================================================================
   The three largest monthly deficits ever recorded, and the smallest
   in six years, all fall inside this episode. Here they are with the
   gold taken out:

        month   reported     gold   adjusted   gold %
   --------------------------------------------------
   2025-01      124.7     32.5       92.2     26.1
   2025-02      117.1     22.3       94.8     19.0
   2025-03      133.0     12.2      120.8      9.2
   2025-10       37.4    -15.2       52.6    -40.6

   On the reported series the record book reads Mar 2025, Jan 2025
   and Feb 2025 - the top three deficits since the series
   begins in 1992, consecutive, all in the episode.

   Adjusted, only 1 of those three still clears the pre-episode
   record of $95.5bn in Mar 2022: Mar 2025.
   Two of the three record months were records because of metal that
   was in a vault in London in the morning and a vault in New York by
   the evening.

   That comparison uses unadjusted figures before October 2023, which
   is where the gold series starts. Monthly net gold ran between -$3bn
   and +$2bn in the year before the episode, so it is indicative rather
   than exact, and the direction is not in doubt.

==============================================================================
STEP 2  The month the deficit 'collapsed'
==============================================================================
   October 2025 printed a deficit of $37.4bn, the smallest since
   November 2019 and widely read as a structural improvement.
   With the gold put back it is $52.6bn. 41% of the
   'improvement' was bullion leaving the country.

   This is the symmetry that matters. The same mechanism that produced
   three record deficits produced, seven months later, the best trade
   month in six years. Neither was trade.

==============================================================================
STEP 3  It does not net out quickly
==============================================================================
   A round trip nets to nothing eventually. The question is how long
   'eventually' takes, and the answer is longer than a year - so the
   annual deficit was wrong too, not just the monthly one.

   Rolling 12-month deficit, overstatement from gold:

      peak        $79bn in March 2025, 7.4% of the trailing-year deficit
      latest      $16bn in November 2025, 1.6%

   At the March 2025 peak every tonne had gone in and none had come
   back, so a full year of data carried the whole surge and none of the
   reversal. 11 months after the metal started moving the twelve-month
   deficit was STILL overstated by $16bn, and the series ends there
   rather than at zero.

   So the reassuring version of this - 'it nets out over a year, only
   the monthly print was distorted' - is wrong, and it is worth saying
   plainly because it is the first thing anyone reaches for. For most
   of 2025 the twelve-month deficit was overstated by tens of billions.

   The monthly series is distorted in a second way that survives any
   netting - how volatile the trade balance appeared to be. Standard
   deviation of the monthly deficit over these 26 months:
      reported   $21.6bn
      adjusted   $13.6bn
   Taking the gold out cuts it by 37%. Range high to low: reported $96bn,
   adjusted $68bn.

==============================================================================
WHAT THIS MEANS FOR HOW THE DEFICIT WAS READ
==============================================================================
   Nothing here is a claim that BEA published a wrong number. The gold
   crossed the border and the balance of payments is supposed to record
   goods crossing borders. The point is narrower and harder to dismiss:

   a reader of the monthly release in early 2025 saw a deficit
   exploding to successive records, and in late 2025 saw it collapsing
   to a six-year low. Neither movement was a change in what America
   buys or sells. It was one pile of metal moving to a different vault
   and then moving back, and the reader had no way to see that from the
   headline figure.

   BEA already takes this view for GDP: nonmonetary gold is removed
   from the national accounts because a bar bought as a store of value
   is not consumption or investment. The trade release applies no such
   filter, so the same metal is excluded from one official statistic
   and headlined in another.
