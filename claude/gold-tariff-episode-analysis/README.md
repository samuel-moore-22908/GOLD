# Gold tariff episode analysis

A ~29-page working memorandum reconstructing the 2024–26 gold tariff episode from primary
sources, written as a sounding board for a shorter RA publication.

## Headline findings

1. **1,381.5 tonnes of gross Swiss–US gold trade, −1.5 tonnes of net position change**, over
   Oct 2024 – Jul 2026. Gross-to-net ratio ≈ 951×. Because the gold price rose ~65% over the
   window, the same metal returning to its origin registers as a **$17.2 bn bilateral goods
   deficit**.
2. **The price signal was small; the flow was large.** Measured as an annualised excess over
   carry, the tariff episode ran at 1.16% against COVID-2020's 7.60% — roughly one-seventh —
   and yet moved more metal per month. Normalised, the tariff episode moved ~8× more metal per
   unit of dislocation. The August 2025 window had the *widest* dollar basis of the whole era
   ($102.20) and almost no flow.
3. **Three customs authorities classify the same bar three different ways** — Switzerland as
   unwrought (7108.12), the UK as semi-manufactured (7108.13), the US as an article of precious
   metal (7115.90) until mid-2025. The US reclassification is datable to CBP ruling `N351466`
   (31 Jul 2025) and is directly visible in the trade data within the same month.
4. **Underneath, unrelated trends were running**: Chinese retail bar-and-coin demand rose 2.5×
   to a record 206.9 t in Q1 2026; the re-export share of US gold exports broke structurally in
   **2022**, not 2024; global ETF holdings accumulated continuously throughout.
5. **Silver corroborates independently** — the same London drawdown signature, five times deeper
   in proportional terms (−16.9% vs gold's −3.4%).

## Things the memorandum corrects

Three claims this project previously carried did not survive the primary data, and the paper
says so explicitly:

- **The basis does not lead the flow by a month.** The correlation is strongest
  contemporaneously (r = +0.55) and decays with lag. Outside the two crisis episodes it falls
  to +0.27 and is flat across lags — it is a crisis-regime relationship, not a general
  leading indicator.
- **The GBR↔CHE mirror mismatch is not unexplained.** At annual frequency the corridor
  reconciles within 0.79–1.02 every year; the monthly disagreement is shipment timing, sitting
  on top of a genuine subheading-classification difference.
- **"London was drained" overstates it.** The drawdown was 3.4%, fully reversed by June 2025,
  and London is now at a record.

## Reproducing

Section 12 of the memorandum is a standalone replication guide — data sources with access
routes, every transformation applied, and the exact recipe for each headline number. It is
written to be followed without reference to the code.

The scripts behind it, in `src/`:

| Script | Produces |
|---|---|
| `build_efp_from_databento.py` | `comex_contract_daily.csv`, `efp_dislocation_v2.csv` |
| `analysis_tariff_episode.py` | `analysis_results.json` — every number cited in the paper |
| `make_paper_figures.py` | the seven figures |

Plus the source-specific pulls already in `src/` (`pull_clean_che_trade.py`,
`clean_us_trade_data.py`, `pull_clean_gbr_trade.py`, `pull_clean_ind_trade.py`,
`pull_comex_warehouse_stocks.py`, `pull_event_dates.py`, `pull_usgs_*.py`).

Data itself is not committed, per the repository policy in the root `README.md`.

## Known limitations

Listed in full in Section 11. The two that most constrain the conclusions:

- **The COMEX baseline is unobserved.** No archived snapshot exists between Apr 2023 and Jan
  2025, so no "build since the election" figure is quoted anywhere in the paper; all COMEX
  deltas run between observed dates.
- **The EFP is a synthetic proxy**, not a dealer quote. Episode rankings are robust to this;
  specific dollar levels are indicative.
