STEP 1  The number the market quotes:  b = F - S
==============================================================================
   mean   $   8.64/oz
   sd     $  16.63/oz
   range  $-236.75 to $ 112.95

   If New York is dear relative to London by more than it costs to ship
   metal, metal ships. That is the intuition the paper needs. But this
   number cannot carry it, for three reasons.

==============================================================================
STEP 2  Why it breaks
==============================================================================
   (a) It is denominated in a unit that is not comparable over time.
       2015: gold $1,160, spread sd $ 4.17   (0.1% of spot = $1.16)
       2026: gold $4,574, spread sd $39.81   (0.1% of spot = $4.57)
       gold x3.9, dispersion x9.6. The same proportional gap is
       worth 3.9 times the dollars at the end of the sample as at
       the start, so pooling the two pools incomparable units.
       corr(spread, gold price) = +0.402

   (b) It contains carry, and carry moves with the delivery calendar.
       The front contract's horizon cycles from about two months to zero
       and resets. Carry is proportional to it, so the spread inherits a
       sawtooth made by the calendar and nothing else:
       tau (0, 15]      mean spread $  2.79   (245 days)
       tau (15, 30]     mean spread $  3.02   (590 days)
       tau (30, 45]     mean spread $  6.99   (553 days)
       tau (45, 60]     mean spread $  8.08   (603 days)
       tau (60, 90]     mean spread $ 12.40   (532 days)
       tau (90, 200]    mean spread $ 20.60   (334 days)
       corr(spread, days to delivery) = +0.327

   (c) It mixes a level with a rate. Location, bar form and counterparty
       trust are paid once. Financing, storage and the forgone lease rate
       accrue per day. Any single summary of b must pick a horizon at
       which to compare them, and the pick decides the answer.

   So b is a function of the horizon, not a variable. The object the
   paper needs is a feature of that function.

==============================================================================
STEP 3  Estimate carry instead of assuming it
==============================================================================
   Each day the listed contracts trace a curve. Fitting
       ln F(tau_i) = a + b*tau_i          weighted by open interest
   splits it into a level and a rate, and the rate is the market's own
   carry - revealed by prices, not asserted.

   contracts in the fit : median 6 a day (range 4-9)
   fit quality          : median R2 0.9987, residual 2.7 bp of price
   fitted carry         : mean 2.75% a year, correlation with SOFR 0.952

   No interest rate enters that fit. Carry tracking SOFR at 0.95 is
   therefore a check on the projection, not an input to it.

==============================================================================
STEP 4  The spread:  p_hat = a - ln S
==============================================================================
   The intercept is the curve extrapolated to zero horizon: the price
   today of New-York-deliverable metal. Against London spot, that is
   the premium - a level, in per cent of spot.

   Carry has gone, because it loads on the horizon and the intercept
   does not. The test is whether the delivery calendar still shows:
       corr(quoted spread, days to delivery) = +0.327
       corr(premium,       days to delivery) = -0.046
   The sawtooth is gone. That is the whole point of the projection.

   The New York leg is read at the London auction rather than at its own
   settlement three and a half hours later, which removed 55% of the
   daily variance: sd 0.499 -> 0.225 pp of spot.

   usable days          : 2,820 of 2,857 (98.7%)
      not re-timed      : 5 (market holidays)
      curve fit broken  : 32 (all in 2020)
   Both are flagged rather than dropped, so a user can choose.
