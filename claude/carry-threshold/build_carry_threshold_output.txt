STEP 1  Carry cost, over the interval the quoted spread spans
==============================================================================
   The quoted spread b is the active contract's price over London spot,
   so it spans that contract's horizon. The carry rate from the fitted
   slope, compounded over the same horizon, is what the spread should be
   if New York and London were the same place:

       K(tau) = S * (exp(carry_rate * tau/365) - 1)

   horizon      : median 46 days (range 7-135)
   carry cost   : mean $  8.75/oz, max $ 65.48
   quoted spread: mean $  8.50/oz
   excess       : mean $ -0.25/oz

   Carry is most of the quoted spread. At 2026 prices and rates it runs
   to tens of dollars an ounce, which is why the raw number is useless
   as a signal and why subtracting it is the whole exercise.

==============================================================================
STEP 2  Check: the excess should be the premium
==============================================================================
   Two routes to the same object. The excess subtracts a carry cost from
   one contract's spread. The premium is the intercept of the curve fitted
   across all of them, re-timed to the London auction. If the
   decomposition is sound they should agree.

   compared with                                  corr   sd of diff
   the premium before re-timing (same settle)    0.998        $0.78
   the premium re-timed to the auction           0.276       $12.14

   The identity holds exactly: 0.998, agreeing to 78 cents an ounce,
   which is the difference between reading one contract and fitting the
   whole curve. The decomposition is sound.

   The second line is the interesting one, and it is not a failure of
   the identity. The quoted spread is struck at the New York settlement
   and London spot three and a half hours earlier, so the excess carries
   the whole of that timing error:
       sd of the premium itself     $ 3.95/oz
       sd of the timing error       $12.14/oz
       correlation this implies      0.309   observed 0.276

   averaged monthly    corr 0.737   (140 periods)
   averaged quarterly  corr 0.822   (47 periods)

   This is the practical finding of the whole exercise. Run the
   cash-and-carry test on the quoted spread, the textbook way, and the
   noise is $12 an ounce against a shipping cost of about $3.
   The thing being measured is a third the size of the error in
   measuring it, so the test cannot be run that way at daily frequency
   at all. It needs the re-timed premium, or monthly averaging, or both.

==============================================================================
STEP 3  The threshold: what it costs to move an ounce
==============================================================================
   kappa = $3.00 to $3.77 an ounce.

   This is an assumption and the weakest number in the exercise. Freight,
   insurance and recasting quotes are private; the band is the hinge
   estimated from flows in earlier work. The one public component is the
   COMEX delivery-out charge of $0.35 an ounce, about a tenth of it.

   Note what is NOT added on top. Financing the metal from purchase to
   delivery is already inside the carry cost subtracted in step 1, so
   adding transit financing again would double-count it. What is missing
   is the lease income forgone while metal is in the air, which needs a
   lease rate the project does not have.

   days clearing $3.00: 296 of 2,820 (10.5%)
   days clearing $3.77: 227 of 2,820 (8.0%)

   The band is crossed rarely. That is the point: for most of eleven
   years arbitrage holds the premium inside the cost of acting on it,
   and nothing moves.

STEP 4  Does metal actually move?
==============================================================================
   Swiss customs exports to the United States, tonnes a month. Switzerland
   is where London 400 oz bars are recast into the bars COMEX accepts, so
   this is the physical leg of the westward arbitrage.

   139 months with both a spread and a customs figure

   Months grouped by whether the premium cleared the threshold:
   group                                 n  median t   mean t   max t
   never cleared                        82       2.3      4.7    32.0
   cleared on 1-4 days                  36       4.7     12.9   112.4
   cleared on 5 or more days            21      14.8     47.1   195.4

   correlation, monthly pressure vs tonnes : 0.681
   correlation, mean premium  vs tonnes    : 0.632

   The pressure measure is the daily hinge averaged over the month, not
   the hinge of the monthly average: max(0, x) is convex, so averaging
   first would understate a month with a few days far above the
   threshold and many below it.

   The relationship is not stable across the sample. Splitting the
   months that cleared at the April 2025 tariff exemption:
                              n  median t   median pressure $   t per $
   before Apr 2025           15      44.5                1.82      24.4
   after Apr 2025             6       5.8                3.07       1.9

   The same premium stops producing the same shipments. August 2025 had
   a premium above $10 an ounce on sixteen days and 2.7 tonnes arrived;
   January 2025 had $13.64 and 195 tonnes came. The price signal did not
   weaken, the response to it did.

   This is a finding about the quantity side, not a defect of the
   threshold. The candidate explanation is inventory: COMEX registered
   stock rose sharply through 2025, and an arbitrage can be closed by
   warranting metal already in New York rather than importing any. That
   produces no customs record at all, which is exactly the asymmetry
   CLAUDE.md flags as the project's deepest open problem. Confirming it
   needs the daily warehouse series, which the project does not yet have.
