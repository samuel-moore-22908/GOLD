
==============================================================================
WHAT THE GOLD DID TO MEASURED GDP
==============================================================================

==============================================================================
STEP 1  US gold trade, from the customs universe
==============================================================================
   US imports and exports of HS 7108 and 7115, all partners, monthly.
   Both headings are needed. Over the surge months the bars went in
   under 7115, not 7108:

        month   7108 imports   7115 imports   $bn
   2024-11           2.16           2.18
   2024-12           3.19          10.30
   2025-01           3.79          30.44
   2025-02           2.45          24.73
   2025-03           0.68          17.12

   Anyone pulling 7108 alone, as this project's own build order said
   to, would find almost none of this episode. That is a correction to
   the instruction in CLAUDE.md, not a detail.

   External check on the construction. The Atlanta Fed, describing why
   it rebuilt GDPNow, put nonmonetary gold imports at $13.2bn in
   December 2024 and $32.6bn in January 2025 on a balance-of-payments
   basis. The two headings together give $13.5bn and $34.2bn.
   Close enough that this series is measuring the same thing they are;
   the small gap is the Census-to-BOP adjustment.

   26 months, 2023-10 to 2025-11.
   Value is converted to tonnes at the month's LBMA PM benchmark,
   because a quantity is what the real-GDP arithmetic needs and the
   gold price rose 40% inside this window.

==============================================================================
STEP 2  Quarterly net gold imports
==============================================================================
   Imports minus exports. Positive means metal arriving, which is what
   subtracts from GDP; negative means it leaving, which adds.

     quarter    net $bn   net tonnes   gold $/oz
   2023Q4       -0.4           -8       1,976
   2024Q1       -6.1          -92       2,072
   2024Q2       -1.4          -19       2,338
   2024Q3        0.6            9       2,478
   2024Q4       13.3          156       2,662
   2025Q1       67.0          740       2,862
   2025Q2      -32.7         -313       3,279
   2025Q3       -3.0          -22       3,459

   Complete quarters only; the customs pull ends November 2025, so later
   quarters are dropped rather than annualised from part of a quarter.

==============================================================================
STEP 3  Turning that into a GDP growth contribution
==============================================================================
   The NIPA arithmetic. A component's contribution to annualised real
   GDP growth is its change in quantity, valued at the previous
   quarter's price, over the previous quarter's nominal GDP:

       contribution (pp) = -1600 * (dQ_t * P_t-1) / GDP_t-1

   The minus is because this is an import. The 1600 is 400 for
   annualising a quarterly rate in percent, times 4 because the trade
   figures are quarterly totals and GDP is an annual rate.

   Before trusting it on gold, run the same formula on total imports
   and check it against BEA's own published contribution:

     quarter  this formula  BEA published     gap
   2023Q4           nan          -0.73     nan
   2024Q1         -0.45          -0.40   -0.05
   2024Q2         -1.66          -1.47   -0.19
   2024Q3         -1.73          -1.53   -0.20
   2024Q4         -0.16          -0.13   -0.03
   2025Q1         -4.89          -4.31   -0.58
   2025Q2          5.32           4.71    0.61
   2025Q3         -0.01           0.01   -0.02

   Mean absolute gap 0.24pp. The residual is chain weighting:
   BEA's Fisher index reweights every quarter and this does not. It is
   small enough that the gold figure below is not an artefact of the
   approximation.

==============================================================================
STEP 4  The answer, quarter by quarter
==============================================================================
   The gold term is what net gold trade contributes to a GDP growth
   figure built straight off the trade data. Published GDP does not
   contain it - step 5 shows why - so 'passed through' is published
   growth PLUS the gold term, which is the number a naive trade-driven
   estimate prints.

     quarter  published     gold  passed through   imports   invent   GDPNow
                  % ann       pp           % ann        pp       pp    % ann
   --------------------------------------------------------------------------
   2024Q1       1.44     0.30            1.75     -0.40    -1.04      2.7
   2024Q2       3.58    -0.27            3.31     -1.47     1.21      2.6
   2024Q3       3.15    -0.11            3.04     -1.53    -0.06      2.8
   2024Q4       2.40    -0.63            1.77     -0.13    -0.91      2.3
   2025Q1       0.14    -2.67           -2.53     -4.31     2.54     -2.7
   2025Q2       4.02     5.14            9.16      4.71    -3.17      2.9
   2025Q3       3.88    -1.60            2.28      0.01    -0.14      3.5

   THE TWO QUARTERS THAT MATTER

   2025Q1  gold is worth -2.67pp. Published growth was +0.14%; passed
           through it would have printed -2.53%. The direction is
           DOWNWARD: arriving metal is an import, and imports subtract.
           A phantom import surge makes a trade-driven estimate too
           weak, not too strong.

   2025Q2  the metal went home. Gold is worth +5.14pp, a swing of
           +7.81pp between the two quarters. Published growth was
           +4.02%; passed through, +9.16%. THIS is the over-statement
           the question was after, and it is a full quarter later than
           the surge everyone looked at.

   Gold is 58% of the entire rise in US goods imports in
   2025Q1, on the same Census pull for numerator and denominator. That
   comparison is deliberately NOT made against BEA's published import
   contribution, which already has the gold taken out of it.

   Over the four quarters 2024Q4 to 2025Q3 the gold terms sum to +0.23pp.
   A round trip nets out in the level. It does not net out in any
   single quarter's growth rate, and quarterly growth rates are what
   policy reacts to.

==============================================================================
STEP 5  None of this reaches published GDP
==============================================================================
   BEA does not let nonmonetary gold into the national accounts at all.
   From its own FAQ: ITA exports and imports of nonmonetary gold are
   REMOVED and replaced with an adjustment for gold computed as the
   difference between domestic production and industrial use. Gold
   bought as a store of value is a valuable, and valuables are outside
   consumption, investment and government spending by construction.
   This is long standing - NIPA Handbook chapter 8, reconciliation
   table 4.3C - not a response to 2025.

   The check that it is live rather than nominal is SILVER. BEA's
   Survey of Current Business for 2025Q1 records that it 'identified
   and removed an increase in imports of silver bars in the first
   quarter'. Silver bars arrive under industrial supplies and materials,
   where the standing gold adjustment does not reach, so they had to be
   taken out by hand. Gold needed no mention because gold comes out as
   a matter of course.

   WHAT THIS CORRECTS. An earlier version of this script said the gold
   entered GDP as an import offset by a matching inventory build, and
   pointed at the 2025Q1 inventory contribution as evidence the offset
   worked. That mechanism was wrong. BEA strips the gold out before it
   reaches GDP rather than offsetting it afterwards, and the inventory
   swing in those quarters is broad tariff front-running with nothing
   to do with gold:

   2025Q1   imports -4.31pp   inventories +2.54pp
   2025Q2   imports +4.71pp   inventories -3.17pp

   So the numbers in step 4 are not a claim about published GDP. They
   are what a GDP figure built off raw trade data would show, and the
   place that actually happened is the nowcast.

==============================================================================
STEP 6  The nowcast bridged from a series BEA does not use
==============================================================================
   Here is the bug, stated exactly. GDPNow targets the BEA advance
   estimate, which EXCLUDES nonmonetary gold. It bridged to it from BOP
   goods imports, which INCLUDE nonmonetary gold. The model was mapping
   a trade series into a GDP concept that does not contain what the
   trade series contains, and for a decade that did not matter because
   monthly gold imports ran a billion or two.

   Atlanta Fed GDPNow, final 2025Q1 reading   -2.73%
   Actual 2025Q1 real GDP growth              +0.14%
   Miss                                       -2.87pp
   Gold term computed here                    -2.67pp

   The direct test of the correction in step 5: if published growth is
   already gold-free, then adding the gold term back should reconstruct
   what the unadjusted nowcast actually printed.

      published +0.14%  +  gold -2.67pp  =  -2.53%
      GDPNow, standard model, final vintage   =  -2.73%
      gap 0.20pp

   That is a reconstruction of a number built by a different model on
   different data, from customs figures and an accounting identity, and
   it lands a fifth of a point away. It would not work if published GDP
   still contained the gold, which is the check on step 5.

   BUT DO NOT READ IT AS 'GOLD EXPLAINS THE WHOLE MISS'. Note first
   that this gap and the miss-minus-gold-term gap are the SAME number:
   nowcast - (published + gold) is identically (nowcast - published) -
   gold. One comparison, not two pieces of evidence.

   And it cannot be the whole story, because the Atlanta Fed's own
   gold-adjusted model - which does strip gold out of the trade inputs
   - still read -1.50% at the final vintage against an advance
   estimate of -0.28%. Had gold been the entire miss, their adjusted
   model would have landed on it. It did not.

   The defensible reading has three parts:

     the gold effect on the nowcast is AT LEAST their wedge, 1.2-2.3pp;
     2.67pp is an upper bound - what gold is worth to an
     estimate that passes the trade data straight through;
     the remainder is ordinary forecast error, and at ~1.2pp it is
     unremarkable against GDPNow's 1.20pp RMSE over 49 quarters.

   For scale on the whole episode: 2025Q1's final-vintage error of
   +2.46pp is the fourth largest in GDPNow's history and the largest
   outside the three pandemic quarters of 2020.

   The two adjustments are also not the same object. The Atlanta Fed
   subtracts gold from the trade aggregates its bridge equations
   extrapolate from, so an unrepeatable spike is not carried into the
   months of the quarter not yet observed; observed gold still enters
   GDP. The term computed here removes gold's arithmetic contribution
   outright. The second is the bigger cut by construction, which is
   why -2.67pp exceeds their 1.2-2.3pp wedge rather than contradicting
   it.

   That is the claim in CLAUDE.md - phantom flows contaminated a
   Federal Reserve GDP nowcast - reproduced from primary data. It is a
   replication, not a discovery: the Atlanta Fed diagnosed it at the
   time, recalibrated GDPNow for the gold distortion between 28
   February and 6 March 2025, and then published two versions of the
   nowcast, standard and gold-adjusted, from 6 March through April.
   The value of redoing it here is that the same excess-trade estimate
   now carries a cost figure and a GDP figure on one set of numbers.
