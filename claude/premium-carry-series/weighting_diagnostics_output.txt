==============================================================================
WHAT THIS MEANS FOR THE WEIGHTS
==============================================================================
The inverse-variance case for weighting by liquidity is not supported in
this panel: residual dispersion is flat across open-interest quintiles,
flat across volume quintiles, and the same for contracts that traded and
contracts that did not, once leverage is accounted for.

That does not make weighting wrong, but it changes what it is for. Open
interest tilts the fit towards near-dated contracts, which is where the
intercept is extrapolated to, so it reduces the variance of the
extrapolation. That is an argument about leverage and influence, not
about precision, and it is the one the paper should make.

Volume carries the one thing open interest cannot: whether a settlement
is a traded price at all. Untraded settlements are not noisier, but they
are not independent either, so they flatter the fit statistics. Reporting
the intercept's standard error on traded contracts only is the honest
robustness check, and volume is what makes it possible.
