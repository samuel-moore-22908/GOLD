EXCESS GOLD TRADE: THE AREA BETWEEN ACTUAL AND A PRE-TREND LINE
==========================================================================
   Gold shipped to the United States from Switzerland and the United
   Kingdom, the two places it comes from when the arbitrage runs. Both
   are the exporters' own customs figures, because US import figures
   carry no mass.

   pre-period   2015-01 to 2024-10, 118 months, mean 11.1 t a month
   The line is fitted to the whole series before the break, not a
   recent slice, so the baseline is not chosen to flatter the result.
   fitted line  +0.109 tonnes a month, reaching 17.8 t by January 2025

                     actual   baseline    excess
   episode, t           734         89       645
   episode, $bn        65.5        5.5      60.0
   to date, t           896        372       524
   to date, $bn        81.8       23.3      58.4

   The headline is the whole area after the break: 524 tonnes over 20 months.
   12 of them fall below the line and net off -171
   tonnes; counting only the months above it would give 695 t.

   TONNES NET CLEANLY; DOLLARS DO NOT. Metal went west at about
   $2,778 an ounce during the episode and came back east later at well
   over four thousand, so a dollar figure netted across the whole window
   depends more on the price path than on the trade:
      fitting a separate line to the value series   $  58.4bn
      valuing the excess tonnes month by month      $  41.3bn
   Those differ by more than a third for the same 524 tonnes, which is a
   sign the object is ill-defined rather than that one method is wrong.

   So the dollar figure quoted is the episode: $60.0bn of excess over the
   five months to March 2025, at the $2,778 an ounce those shipments
   actually moved at. That is also the number that matters for the trade
   statistics, which record gross flows rather than net ones - a tonne
   leaving later adds to exports, it does not subtract from imports.

   Starting the pre-period elsewhere moves it, but not much over the
   episode:
      from 2015: slope +0.109 t a month, episode excess 645 t
      from 2019: slope -0.220 t a month, episode excess 693 t
      from 2021: slope -0.187 t a month, episode excess 699 t

   For scale, over the same five months: $60bn of excess against a goods
   deficit of $615bn, so 10% of it, and 4.1% of imports.

   None of it was consumed or bought in any economic sense, and a fifth
   went back out within five months - but it enters the trade balance at
   full value.
